Changing the census map area

CBIO Cardiometabolic Disease Register. A guide for the Administrator. Build 0.27.

What this is for

The census map shows every household the Enumerators have recorded as a small square, on a background of buildings and roads. The app comes with a background for Nima and Mamobi in Accra. If your project works somewhere else, or covers more ground, you replace that background with a map of your own area. This guide shows how.

You need: the Administrator sign-in, and an internet connection (Wi-Fi is best). It takes about five minutes.

When to change the map area

  • The project is in a different neighbourhood, town or country.
  • The project area has grown beyond the edge of the map.
  • Household squares appear on a blank background, and the map page says that some households lie outside the map area.

Change the map before the census starts if you can. It is safe to change it later too: the households already recorded, and their squares, do not change. Only the background changes.

Step 1. Open the Census map page

Sign in as the Administrator and tap Census map at the top of the screen.

Scroll down below the map to the section called Census map area. It shows which map is in use now, and has the buttons you need.

Step 2. Draw a rectangle around the project area

  1. Tap Choose a new map area. A map opens.
  2. Move the map with your finger, and zoom with the + and − buttons, until you can see the whole project area.
  3. Tap Draw the rectangle. The button changes to "Now drag on the map…".
  4. Put your finger on one corner of the project area and drag to the opposite corner. A red rectangle appears. Lift your finger.
  5. If the rectangle is wrong, tap Draw the rectangle and drag again.
Tips. Leave a small margin around the project area, about one street wide. If dragging is hard on your tablet, zoom until the project area fills the map and tap Use the area now on the screen instead.

The rectangle must not be larger than about 25 square kilometres (for example 5 km by 5 km). A larger area would be too slow for the map service and too heavy for the tablets. If your rectangle is too large you will see this message, and the Fetch button will stay grey. Draw a smaller rectangle.

Step 3. Fetch the map

Tap Fetch the map. The app asks the OpenStreetMap service for the buildings and roads inside your rectangle. This can take up to a minute. Keep the screen open and wait.

Step 4. Check the preview

NumberWhat it means
Buildings foundHow many building outlines OpenStreetMap has in your rectangle. In a town you should see hundreds or thousands. A very small number means that the area is not well mapped (see below).
Roads and paths foundHow many streets, lanes and footpaths were found.
AreaThe size of the map, in square kilometres.
Size for each tabletHow much each tablet will download, once. Under 1 MB is small. Above 3 MB, make sure the tablets are on Wi-Fi.

Look at the map itself. Move and zoom it. Check that the project area is all there and that streets you know are in the right places.

If the app warns "Very few buildings were found": OpenStreetMap volunteers have not yet drawn the buildings in this area. You can still use the map. Household squares will appear in the right places, but with little background behind them. You do not need to do anything else.

If the map looks right, tap Use this map. If not, tap Cancel: nothing changes.

Step 5. Ask every worker to sync on Wi-Fi

The new map is saved in the project's database. Each tablet downloads it once, the next time it syncs, and then keeps it so that the map works with no connection.

The download can be a few megabytes. On mobile data that costs money and can fail halfway. So after you change the map:

  1. Tell the Promoters and the Enumerators that the map has changed.
  2. Ask each of them to connect the tablet to Wi-Fi and wait until the top of the screen says Synced.
  3. They then open Census map (Enumerators: Map) once to check that the new background is there.

If the fetch fails

The OpenStreetMap map service is free and shared by many people, and it is often busy. The app tries two servers. If neither answers:

  1. Check the internet connection by opening any website.
  2. Wait a few minutes and tap Fetch the map again. Your rectangle is still there.
  3. If it still fails after several tries, or later in the day, use a map file as described next.

The other way: upload a map file

You can make the map file yourself on a computer, and upload it. The result is the same.

  1. On a computer, open overpass-turbo.eu in a web browser.
  2. Move and zoom the map on the right until it shows the whole project area, and nothing much more.
  3. Delete everything in the box on the left, and type or paste these lines exactly: 
    [out:json][timeout:90];
(
  way["building"]({{bbox}});
  way["highway"]({{bbox}});
);
out geom;
  4. Click Run. If it warns that the result is large, choose to continue.
  5. Click Export, then download as GeoJSON. A file called export.geojson is saved on the computer.
  6. Open the CMD Register on the same computer and sign in as the Administrator. Go to Census map, then Census map area, and click Upload a map file (GeoJSON). Choose the file.
  7. The same preview appears. Check it and click Use this map.

The words {{bbox}} tell overpass-turbo to use the area shown on its map. The 25 square kilometre limit applies to a file too.

Going back to the built-in map

In the Census map area section, tap Restore default map and confirm. The built-in map of Nima and Mamobi comes back. The households do not change. Ask the workers to sync again, as in Step 5.

Good to know

  • Only the Administrator can change the map area. The app records who changed it and when.
  • Changing the map never deletes or moves a household.
  • The map shows buildings as OpenStreetMap had them on the day you fetched it. To pick up newer mapping, choose the same area again.
  • If a worker's tablet still shows the old background, it has not synced since the change. Connect it to Wi-Fi and wait for Synced.
